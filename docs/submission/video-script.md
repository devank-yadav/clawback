# Video script: 3 minutes

> Talking points, not lines to memorize. Know each beat's point, the shot and the number; say it your own way.
> Facts and numbers come from [judging-script.md](judging-script.md), [judge-qa.md](judge-qa.md),
> [sources.md](sources.md) and data/CLAUDE.md. Peak Bar's numbers are fixed: never round them.
>
> **Shape:** who we are (10 s) → the problem, in a store (35 s) → the solution, live (1 min 45 s) → the ask (30 s).
>
> - **The bar on camera is Peak Bar**, not a real brand: wrap any snack bar in a printed Peak Bar Co. label, or keep
>   a real label turned away. Never say a real company's distributor short-paid it.
> - **Say "ours" once** (beat 3): Peak Bar, Northmart and their numbers are ours; the contracts from SEC filings and
>   the court cases are real.
> - **Real companies:** only what the source says ("Omaha Industries sued UNFI in 2024, saying…"). Never what
>   Clawback would have won for them.
> - **Film where you're allowed:** ask the store, or use the hackathon's snack table. Captions on every line.

## The beats

### 1. Who we are (0:00–0:10)

**Shot:** the three of you, side by side, at the venue.

**Points:**
- "Hi, we're Devank, Rishi and Manasv, and this is Clawback."
- One line on what it is: an AI analyst that wins back the money distributors wrongly take from small brands.

**Transition:** one of you holds up a snack bar: "Let us show you where the money goes."

### 2. The problem, in a store (0:10–0:45)

**Shot:** walk to a snack shelf, pick up the Peak Bar Co. bar, turn it to camera. Simple graphics appear over the
shot as each point lands.

**Points, in order:**
1. **The chain.** "This bar is made by a small brand. To reach this shelf, it goes through a big distributor."
   Graphic: brand → distributor → store.
2. **The contract.** "The brand and the distributor sign a promo deal: a dollar off per bar, up to 5,000 bars, a
   display fee at the distributor's discretion." Graphic: the three clauses, §3.1, §3.2 and §5.4.
3. **The short payment.** "Then the distributor pays the brand's invoice short. Peak Bar invoiced $48,200 and got
   $41,350. $6,850 was taken back in six charges, with no explanation." Graphic: the remittance slip, −$6,850 in
   red (the motion piece's Problem card).
4. **Why it sticks.** "Checking each charge against the contract takes hours a small brand doesn't have, so the
   money gets written off."
5. **It's real.** Caption only, no need to say it: *Omaha Industries sued UNFI in 2024, saying UNFI charged back
   $268,816.94 on about $326,780 of product.*

**Transition:** put the bar down, cut to the laptop: "So we built the analyst they don't have."

### 3. The solution, live (0:45–2:30)

Screen recording of clawback.biz, signed in as Dana (Peak Bar's owner). Voice-over.

**3a. The payment arrives (0:45–1:00).** Home → **Check for new payments** → Northmart's payment opens on the
Agents page.
- Say it once: "Peak Bar and Northmart are ours. The AI, the math and the QuickBooks books are real."
- Point at the seven steps: Payment in → Read the deal → Check each charge → File the dispute → Your answer → Your
  approval → QuickBooks. Three are AI, two are plain code, two are the owner.

**3b. Reading the deal (1:00–1:15).** The contract with highlights.
- Two different models, Claude and Gemini, read the same contract separately.
- A term counts only if both read it the same way and the sentence is in the contract word for word.

**3c. Checking each charge (1:15–1:40).** Open C1, then C3.
- C1: they charged $1.50 a bar, the contract says $1.00. Code does the math: $600 disputed, the fair $1,200
  accepted.
- C3 looks wrong, April dates on a March promo, but the brand's approved broker extended it by email. Accepted.
  **It doesn't fight fair charges.**

**3d. The one question (1:40–1:55).** The question card with its risk badge, answered on the owner's phone.
- The display fee is "at its discretion", a judgment call, so it asks the owner, once.
- She answers "accept if notice is at least 14 days." That becomes Rule #1, and next month it won't ask.

**3e. The result (1:55–2:30).** The claw pulls $4,120 into the dispute bar → **Review and send** on D-0417 → the
approval → QuickBooks.
- **$4,120 disputed, $2,730 accepted, one question.** About a minute.
- Each disputed line goes out with its proof: the clause, the sales rows and the math. The owner reviews it first.
- Money is the one thing no agent touches: only the owner approves the $2,730 credit memo, and QuickBooks keeps
  $4,120 open with the dispute lines.

### 4. The ask (2:30–3:00)

**Shot:** Home with Peak Bar's distributors, then back to the three of you, the bar on the table.

**Points:**
- "Peak Bar's other distributors run on real contracts from SEC filings: KeHE, Walmart, Five Below and UNFI. Where
  a number is blacked out, Clawback asks instead of guessing."
- "30 of 30 on planted charges, zero wrong automatic actions."
- Back on the team, holding up the bar: "Every bar on this shelf has a contract behind it. Clawback makes sure the
  brand gets paid what it signed for. Try it at clawback.biz."
